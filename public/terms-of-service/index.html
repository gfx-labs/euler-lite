2. What Poppie Is and How It Works

2.1 Non-Custodial Interface to Euler Markets

We never have custody, possession, or control of your Crypto Assets at any time. You initiate and authorize all transactions through your Wallet; we do not execute transactions on your behalf. The Poppie Markets execute autonomously through the Euler Smart Contracts.

2.2 Borrowing and Lending Mechanics

In the Poppie Markets, you may supply a Borrowable Asset to earn yield, and/or deposit a Tokenized Asset as collateral and borrow a Borrowable Asset against it. Whether and how much you may borrow, and whether your position is subject to Liquidation, are determined by the Euler Smart Contracts based on the value of your collateral and borrowings (as reported by the Oracle), the applicable Risk Parameters, and other protocol mechanics. In addition to the risks described in the Master Terms (including the general Assumption of Risk and DeFi-related risks) and elsewhere in this Agreement, you acknowledge and accept the following risks specific to Poppie. 5.1 Liquidation Risk

If the value of your collateral falls relative to your borrowings (as reported by the Oracle and measured against the applicable liquidation LTV), some or all of your collateral may be liquidated, possibly at a discount and possibly resulting in the loss of a substantial portion or all of your collateral. Liquidation can result from market price movements, from a Tokenized Asset’s price diverging from its underlying security, from changes to the Risk Parameters, or from Oracle behavior. Liquidation may occur at any time, including immediately and without notice.

5.2 Parameter Risk

The Risk Parameters directly affect your borrowing capacity and your exposure to Liquidation, and the Company may change them from time to time. Most changes are subject to a timelock of at least 24 hours and are publicly viewable on-chain during that period; emergency changes, however, are not subject to the timelock and may take effect immediately (see Section 4.4). In each case the Company does not separately notify you. A change in a parameter (for example, a reduction in liquidation LTV or a reduction in a borrow cap) may move a position closer to, or into, Liquidation when it takes effect, and in the case of an emergency change this may occur without any advance period during which you could act. You should monitor the Risk Parameters, including any pending changes, and manage your position accordingly.

5.3 Oracle Risk

The Poppie Markets depend on the Oracle, which the Company operates. The PoppieEuler oracle contract was deployed by the Company and has been independently audited and tested. Smart-contract risk nonetheless cannot be eliminated; audits reduce but do not remove the possibility of a bug or vulnerability, and the residual probability of a contract-level exploit is low but non-zero.

Beyond the contract, the Oracle relies on off-chain data sources and a keeper process to update reported prices. This is a standing operational surface: prices may be delayed, stale, incorrect, or manipulable, and the data or keeper infrastructure may fail. Because positions remain open over time and Liquidation depends on reported prices, errors or manipulation in the Oracle system can cascade into wrongful Liquidation or bad debt in a way that a single atomic transaction would not be exposed to. The Company takes measures intended to mitigate these risks but cannot eliminate them, and the probability of an exploit or failure is non-zero.

As noted in Section 4.3, there is currently no fallback oracle. If the Oracle fails or misreports, there may be no automatic backstop.

The Poppie Markets are built on Euler, a Third-Party Protocol. Euler’s contracts are mature, have operated at scale for an extended period, hold substantial assets, and have been audited. That history reduces, but does not eliminate, the risk of a protocol-level bug, vulnerability, or exploit; smart-contract protocols (including Euler) have experienced significant exploits in the past, and no protocol can be assured to be free of defects. We do not control Euler and are not responsible for its performance, security, or correctness. See Section 2.3 and the Master Terms (Third-Party Services).
